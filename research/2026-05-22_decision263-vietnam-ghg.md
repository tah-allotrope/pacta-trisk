# Research Brief: Decision 263 and Vietnam's GHG Regulatory Landscape

> **Date:** 2026-05-22
> **Author:** PACTA-TRISK Vietnam project
> **Purpose:** Evidence base for the BIDV Decision 263 compliance mapping document (GAP-02). Covers Decision 263's sector-specific GHG quota regime, the broader regulatory framework, and implications for Vietnamese commercial banks.

---

## 1. Decision 263/QĐ-TTg Overview

**What it is:** Decision 263/QĐ-TTg establishes **pilot greenhouse gas emission quotas** for Vietnam's highest-emitting industrial sectors. It is the key implementing instrument for the carbon market pilot phase.

**Issued by:** Prime Minister of Vietnam

**When:** 2025 (takes effect for the 2025–2027 pilot phase)

**What it mandates:**
- Sets specific GHG emission quotas for **thermal power, steel, and cement** facilities
- These three sectors are the first to be subject to mandatory emission caps
- Forms part of the broader carbon market pilot phase (2025–2027) before full market launch in 2028
- Covered facilities must conduct GHG inventories, report to MONRE, stay within allocated quotas, and develop emission reduction plans

**Source:** Baker McKenzie, "Vietnam's New Carbon Market: What You Need to Know" (July 2024); DFDL, "Vietnam's New GHG Emissions Regulations" (March 2024)

## 2. Legal Foundation

### Law on Environmental Protection 2020

- **Effective:** January 1, 2022
- **Article 91:** Establishes the legal foundation for Vietnam's carbon market
  - Mandates GHG emission inventory and reporting for high-emitting facilities
  - Provides the authority for the Prime Minister to issue decisions on emission quotas (hence Decision 263)
  - Sets the framework for carbon credit exchange and trading

### Implementing Decrees

| Decree | Date | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| Decree 06/2022/ND-CP | Jan 7, 2022 | GHG emission reduction, ozone layer protection, carbon market development |
| Decree 08/2022/ND-CP | Jan 10, 2022 | Voluntary carbon credits |

### Key Provisions of Decree 06

- Facilities emitting **≥3,000 tonnes CO2e/year** (or meeting sector-specific thresholds set by MONRE) must conduct GHG inventories
- Establishes the regulatory framework for monitoring and verification (MRV)
- Defines the pilot phase structure for the carbon market

## 4. GHG Inventory Requirements

**Who must report:**
- Facilities in sectors with high GHG emissions (≥3,000 tCO2e/year per Decree 06)
- Specifically: thermal power plants, steel mills, and cement plants above threshold

**What must be reported:**
- Direct emissions (Scope 1)
- Potentially indirect emissions (Scope 2) depending on sector guidance
- Data submitted to MONRE for verification

**When:**
- **Reporting since 2025** — the pilot phase requires facilities to report their emissions data
- Annual reporting cycle

**Data collection implications for banks:**
- BIDV's Decision 263 clients are **legally required** to collect and report emissions data from 2025
- This means BIDV can request emissions data from these borrowers as part of credit risk assessment — the data collection obligation already exists at the borrower level
- Data feeds into the PACTA+TRISK pipeline via the BYOL intake (`intake/SCHEMA.md`)

## 5. Sector-Specific Emission Quotas

**How quotas work:**
- Decision 263 sets **absolute emission caps** for each covered facility in the three pilot sectors
- Quotas are allocated by MONRE based on facility-specific baseline emissions and sector benchmarks
- Facilities must stay within their allocated quota; excess emissions may require purchasing carbon credits

**Published quota levels:**
- **Cannot independently verify** the exact numerical quota levels for individual sectors/facilities
- These are typically published in Vietnamese-language government gazettes and MONRE circulars
- The quota methodology is based on historical emissions data and sector-specific reduction targets aligned with Vietnam's NDC

**Timeline:**
- **2025–2027:** Pilot phase with quotas for thermal power, steel, cement
- **2028:** Full carbon market launch (exchange and trading of carbon credits)

---

## 6. Emission Reduction Plan Requirements

Beyond reporting, covered entities must:
- **Develop and implement emission reduction plans** aligned with their allocated quotas
- **Monitor and report** emissions annually
- **Participate in the carbon market** once operational (2028+) — buying/selling carbon credits to meet compliance
- **Comply with MRV** (Measurement, Reporting, Verification) requirements under the developing national MRV system

**Bank implication:** BIDV should monitor borrower compliance with emission reduction plans as part of credit risk management. Borrower non-compliance could affect loan performance. TRISK stress testing can model the financial impact of quota-driven transitions on borrower NPV and PD.

## 7. Enforcement Mechanisms

**Compliance monitoring:**
- MONRE (Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment) is the primary enforcement authority
- Decree 06 establishes the regulatory framework for monitoring and verification
- The national MRV system is under development (supported by GIZ)

**Penalties:**
- **Cannot independently verify** specific penalty amounts
- Non-compliance with GHG inventory and reporting obligations carries administrative fines under Vietnam's environmental protection enforcement framework

**Timeline:**
- 2025–2027: Pilot enforcement (learning phase)
- 2028+: Full enforcement with carbon market mechanisms

## 9. SBV Green Taxonomy — Decision 1604/QĐ-NHNN

**What it is:** The State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) issued **Decision 1604/QĐ-NHNN** in **2023** establishing Vietnam's **green taxonomy** for the financial sector.

**Purpose:**
- Classifies economic activities as **green**, **transition**, or **non-green** for lending and investment purposes
- Enables banks to identify and report on green/transition lending portfolios
- Supports SBV's broader green credit guidelines and sustainable finance framework

**Classification framework:**
- Activities are classified based on environmental criteria aligned with international taxonomies (EU Taxonomy, ASEAN Taxonomy)
- Covers multiple sectors including energy, transport, agriculture, construction, and manufacturing
- **Transition activities** are explicitly included — critical for thermal power, steel, and cement sectors undergoing decarbonization

**Bank reporting obligations:**
- Vietnamese banks must classify their loan portfolios according to the taxonomy
- Report green/transition lending volumes to SBV
- Integrate taxonomy into credit risk assessment and lending decisions

**IFC support:** IFC published "Vietnam's Green Taxonomy: A Guide for Financial Institutions" (2024) to help banks implement the taxonomy.

## 10. BIDV's Dual Obligations

### Borrower-Side (Decision 263)

If BIDV lends to thermal power, steel, or cement facilities subject to Decision 263 quotas, those borrowers must:
- Conduct GHG inventories and report to MONRE
- Stay within allocated emission quotas
- Develop emission reduction plans
- Eventually participate in the carbon market (2028+)

**Credit risk implication:** Borrower non-compliance with emission quotas could affect loan performance. BIDV should monitor borrower GHG compliance as part of credit risk management.

### Bank-Side (SBV Taxonomy)

BIDV must:
- Classify its loan portfolio using Decision 1604/QĐ-NHNN taxonomy
- Report green/transition lending to SBV
- Integrate climate risk and taxonomy classification into lending decisions
- Align with SBV's green credit guidelines

### Intersection

- BIDV's borrowers in thermal power, steel, and cement may qualify as **transition activities** under the SBV taxonomy if they have credible decarbonization plans
- BIDV should track both borrower GHG compliance (Decision 263) and taxonomy classification (Decision 1604) for the same portfolio
- This creates a dual reporting obligation: borrower emissions data (for credit risk) and taxonomy classification (for regulatory reporting)

### How PACTA+TRISK Addresses Both

|---|---|---|
| Monitor borrower alignment with decarbonization pathways | Sector-level alignment gaps, technology mix vs. PDP8/NDC/NZE | — |
| Assess financial impact of transition scenarios on borrowers | — | Borrower-level NPV change, PD change under stress scenarios |
| Identify priority borrowers for engagement | Company-level alignment gaps | Priority score ranking |

## 11. Data Availability Assessment

**What emissions data BIDV can expect from Decision 263 clients:**
- Since 2025, Decision 263 clients are **legally required** to collect and report emissions data to MONRE
- This includes Scope 1 emissions (direct) for thermal power, steel, and cement facilities
- BIDV can request this data from borrowers as part of credit risk assessment — the collection obligation already exists at the borrower level
- Data quality will improve over time as the MRV system matures (GIZ-supported)

**How this data feeds into the PACTA+TRISK pipeline:**
- Borrower emissions data → PACTA matching and alignment analysis
- Emissions intensity data → SDA analysis for cement and steel
- Scenario pathways (PDP8, NDC, NZE) → alignment gap calculation
- TRISK stress testing → financial impact assessment under transition scenarios
