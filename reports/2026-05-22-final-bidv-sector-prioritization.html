01 / Executive Summary

Executive Summary

What Was Built

A reusable R script (scripts/sector_prioritization.R) that reads existing PACTA alignment outputs, TRISK stress-test scores, and loanbook exposure data, then computes a three-dimensional composite priority score for each Decision 263 sector (thermal power, cement, steel).

Key Result

Power: 1.000 [Critical] — coal capacity 13.4 pp above PDP8 target, 13 borrowers with stress scores up to 100, 81.8% of Decision 263 exposure. Steel: 0.158 [Low] — moderate SDA gap (7.2%). Cement: 0.011 [Low] — smallest gap (2.1%).

Methodology

Three dimensions, min-max normalized to [0, 1]: alignment gap severity (0.35 weight), transition stress severity (0.35), portfolio exposure concentration (0.30). Composite = weighted sum. Priority bands: Critical ≥ 0.70, High 0.50–0.69, Medium 0.30–0.49, Low < 0.30.

Deliverables

Methodology document (docs/bidv_sector_prioritization_methodology.md), R script, ranking CSV, detail CSV, stacked bar chart, interpretation notes. All 4 phases completed, cross-validated, and committed.

02 / Background & Objective

Background & Objective

Context. The pacta-trisk repo produces sector-level alignment gaps (via PACTA) and borrower-level stress-test scores (via TRISK) independently. The BIDV gap analysis identified GAP-03: no module joins these outputs into a sector prioritization recommendation. BIDV needs to know which Decision 263 sector to tackle first — thermal power, cement, or steel — based on a transparent, auditable scoring methodology.

The gap. The engagement scoring concept from research/future_planning_ideas.md (Idea 3) described a borrower-level composite score (0.5 × alignment + 0.5 × TRISK) but was never implemented and operated at borrower level, not sector level. No script existed to aggregate borrower scores into sector-level priorities or to incorporate portfolio exposure as a third dimension.

Objective. Build a reusable sector prioritization module that: (1) defines a transparent three-dimensional scoring methodology; (2) implements it as an R script consuming only existing pipeline outputs; (3) demonstrates it against the MCB synthetic portfolio; (4) produces report-ready outputs (ranking CSV, detail CSV, chart, interpretation notes) for the BIDV Framework Recommendation Report (GAP-04).

04 / Assumptions & Constraints

Assumptions & Constraints

Assumptions

  • ASM-001: Strictly Decision 263 sectors only (power, cement, steel). Automotive excluded.
  • ASM-002: Default weights: 0.35 alignment, 0.35 stress, 0.30 exposure — near-equal to avoid pre-judging BIDV's risk appetite.
  • ASM-003: TRISK scores aggregated via exposure-weighted mean — a sector with one large stressed borrower is more urgent than many small ones.
  • ASM-004: Min-max normalization across sectors makes different units (pp vs. %) comparable by relative severity.

Constraints

  • CON-001: Script consumes only existing pipeline outputs — no re-running PACTA or TRISK pipelines. Runs as a fast post-processing step.
  • CON-002: All scoring is transparent and auditable — every intermediate value traceable to a source file and row.
  • DEC-001: Demonstration uses MCB synthetic portfolio; outputs include data_source = "MCB_synthetic" to distinguish from future BIDV runs.
05 / Methodology

Methodology

The analysis progressed across four sequential phases:

PHASE-01 — Scoring Methodology. Defined three dimensions with extraction rules and normalization: alignment gap severity (max positive share_gap_pp for power, gap_pct for cement/steel SDA), transition stress severity (exposure-weighted mean of TRISK stress_priority_score), and portfolio exposure concentration (sector share of total Decision 263 exposure). Min-max scaling normalizes all dimensions to [0, 1]. Composite formula: 0.35 × alignment + 0.35 × stress + 0.30 × exposure. Priority bands: Critical (≥ 0.70), High (0.50–0.69), Medium (0.30–0.49), Low (< 0.30). Documented in docs/bidv_sector_prioritization_methodology.md.

PHASE-02 — R Script. Built scripts/sector_prioritization.R (364 lines) with 7 sections: configuration (CLI weight parameters), alignment data loading, TRISK data loading, exposure calculation, dimension scoring with min-max normalization, composite computation and band classification, and output writing (ranking CSV, detail CSV, stacked bar chart via ggplot2).

PHASE-03 — Demonstration. Ran the script against MCB synthetic portfolio outputs. Verified ranking plausibility, detail CSV consistency, and chart quality. Ran sensitivity check with alternative weights (0.50/0.25/0.25) confirming ranking stability. Wrote interpretation notes.

PHASE-04 — Verification. Cross-checked ranking CSV against detail CSV (composite scores match), verified chart base64-encoding (27,976 chars), confirmed output directory compatibility with dashboard refresh script, and updated methodology document with MCB results appendix.

07 / Findings & Recommendation

Findings & Recommendation

Finding 1 — Power dominates all three dimensions. With a composite score of 1.000 (maximum), power is the unequivocal priority sector. Its coal capacity is 13.4 percentage points above the PDP8 target (highest alignment gap), it has 13 borrowers with TRISK stress scores up to 100 (highest stress), and it accounts for 81.8% of Decision 263 exposure (highest concentration). The convergence of all three dimensions at maximum makes this ranking robust to any reasonable weight variation.

Finding 2 — Steel's moderate alignment gap is offset by low stress and exposure. Steel's SDA gap (7.2%) is significantly higher than cement's (2.1%), giving it a normalized alignment score of 0.453. However, its stress score (0.000) and exposure score (0.000) are at or near the floor, limiting its composite to 0.158.

Finding 3 — Cement ranks last despite having two borrowers with divergent stress scores. VICEM has a stress priority score of 95 while Holcim Group has 5, but the exposure-weighted mean is at the floor (100, the minimum in this dataset). Cement's small alignment gap (2.1%) and low exposure share (10.4%) compound to produce the lowest composite score.

Recommendation. BIDV should prioritize the power sector for immediate climate risk management action: borrower engagement with coal-heavy entities (Nghi Son Power, Vinacomin Power, Mong Duong), sector exposure limit review, and board-level reporting. Steel and cement should be included in the quarterly monitoring cycle but do not require immediate action under the current portfolio composition.

09 / Risks / Open Questions

Risks / Open Questions

Key Caveats

  • Steel match coverage ~4%: The MCB synthetic portfolio has very low steel match coverage, making steel alignment and stress results less reliable. Steel's true priority may be higher if additional borrowers were matched.
  • Different alignment units: Power gaps are in percentage points (market share) while cement/steel gaps are in percent (emission intensity). Min-max scaling makes them comparable by relative severity, but absolute magnitude is lost.
  • Synthetic portfolio: All results are based on the synthetic MCB portfolio, not real BIDV data. BIDV-specific results will differ based on actual sector exposure and borrower composition.

Open Questions

  • BIDV's actual sector exposure: If BIDV's portfolio composition differs materially from MCB (e.g., higher cement/steel exposure, lower power exposure), the ranking may shift. The script accepts real data without modification.
  • Weight calibration: The default weights (0.35/0.35/0.30) are illustrative. BIDV should calibrate weights during implementation based on its risk appetite — a compliance-focused bank might weight alignment higher, a credit-risk-focused bank might weight stress higher.
  • Automotive exclusion: Automotive is not a Decision 263 sector and was excluded per the plan's recommended default. If BIDV has significant automotive lending, a separate prioritization module could be built using PACTA alignment data only (TRISK does not cover automotive).
