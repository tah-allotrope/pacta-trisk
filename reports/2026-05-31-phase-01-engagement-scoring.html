03 / Math / Algorithm Used

Math / Algorithm Used

Min-max normalisation (with a zero-range guard returning 0.5, mirroring sector_prioritization.R):

norm(x) = (x - min(x)) / (max(x) - min(x))

applied independently to alignment_gap (across all 23 borrowers) and trisk_priority_score (across the 17 TRISK-covered borrowers).

Composite score (fixed 50/50, Grill Me Q-004), renormalised by the weight actually available so partial rows are not artificially buried:

composite = (w_align·normAlign + w_trisk·normTRISK) / (w_align + w_trisk_present)

  • TRISK-covered row: (0.5·normAlign + 0.5·normTRISK) / 1.0.
  • PACTA-only row (automotive): TRISK term absent → (0.5·normAlign) / 0.5 = normAlign, flagged composite_partial = TRUE.

Automotive borrower gap = mean of absolute per-technology share gaps at 2030 under PDP8: mean(|projected_share − target_pdp8_ndc_share|) × 100 (pp), pivoted from the long-format company file.

06 / Limitations / Second-Best Alternative

Limitations / Second-Best Alternative

  • Cross-sector comparability: power/automotive gaps are market-share percentage-points; cement/steel gaps are SDA emission-intensity gap_pct. After global min-max, cement/steel (2.1 / 7.2) compress near the floor. The composite is a demo prioritisation aid, not a calibrated risk number — documented in the script header and console.
  • Partial-row asymmetry: renormalising automotive to the alignment component lets three automakers (gap 21.95 pp) outrank some TRISK-covered power names. This is intentional (do not penalise a borrower for the pilot's sector scope) and made explicit via composite_partial.
  • Second-best alternative: per-sector normalisation would make magnitudes within-sector fair but loses the single cross-sector ranking the letters/disclosure need. Rejected for v1; revisitable if a bank wants sector-siloed lists.
  • Dung Quat LNG carries a 0.0 alignment gap ("No alignment context available") — the known pre-commissioning zero-baseline edge case from the TRISK pilot, inherited not introduced.
