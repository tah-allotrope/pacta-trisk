02 / Background & Objective

Background & Objective

Context. BIDV, Vietnam’s largest state-owned commercial bank, faces a dual regulatory imperative. First, Decision 263 (2025) establishes mandatory GHG emission quotas for thermal power, steel, and cement facilities — sectors that constitute a material share of BIDV’s lending book, with 20+ clients already mandated to report emissions. Second, the SBV Green Taxonomy (Decision 1604/QĐ-NHNN, 2023) requires banks to classify their portfolios as green, transition, or non-green and report to the central bank.

The problem. The pacta-trisk repo had mature analytical infrastructure — a working PACTA alignment pipeline for power, cement, and steel; a package-backed TRISK stress-test covering all three Decision 263 sectors; an interactive scenario builder; and a deployed Streamlit dashboard. But it contained no content evaluating PACTA and TRISK against peer frameworks, no mapping of analytical outputs to regulatory requirements, and no advisory narrative that a bank’s senior risk management or ESG team could act on. The repo was a demonstration tool, not a client deliverable.

Objective. Close the three critical gaps (GAP-01, GAP-02, GAP-07) identified in the BIDV gap analysis by producing: (1) a structured framework comparison matrix with scored evaluation of 7 leading frameworks across dimensions relevant to BIDV; (2) a Decision 263 compliance mapping showing how the repo’s PACTA+TRISK outputs directly support BIDV’s regulatory obligations. These deliverables form the intellectual core of the final BIDV Framework Recommendation Report (GAP-04).

05 / Methodology

Methodology

The analysis progressed across four sequential phases, with research parallelized where possible:

PHASE-01 — Domain Research. Three parallel subagents researched: (1) PCAF, SBTi FI, and GFANZ; (2) NGFS, TCFD/IFRS S2, and IFC Performance Standards; (3) Decision 263 regulatory texts, SBV green taxonomy, and BIDV’s dual obligations. Each framework was documented across 11 consistent dimensions (purpose, methodology type, data inputs, sector coverage, output types, cost, open-source availability, time to first results, Vietnam relevance, and institutional adoption). The two research briefs served as the evidence base for all downstream documents.

PHASE-02 — Framework Comparison Matrix. The 7 frameworks were scored across 10 evaluation dimensions on a 3-level scale (Strong Fit / Partial Fit / Weak Fit). Each score includes a one-line justification anchored to BIDV’s specific context. Framework-by-framework narratives (~200–400 words each) cover what the framework does, how it works, what BIDV would need, and its specific strengths and weaknesses. The recommendation section presents a three-layer stack with rationale tied to matrix scores.

PHASE-03 — Decision 263 Compliance Mapping. The mapping document traces each Decision 263 requirement (emission inventory, sector-specific quotas, emission reduction plans) to specific repo capabilities, pipeline outputs, and data files. The sector mapping table aligns Decision 263 sectors → PACTA sectors/technologies → TRISK sectors/technologies → repo data files. A three-layer compliance stack architecture (Measure → Compare → Act) shows the logical flow from regulatory requirement to analytical output.

PHASE-04 — Cross-Validation. Both documents were cross-checked for consistency: regulatory compatibility scores in the framework comparison were verified against the Decision 263 mapping evidence; sector and technology names were checked against docs/trisk_multisector_contract.md; and all cross-references were confirmed bidirectional.

07 / Findings & Recommendation

Findings & Recommendation

Finding 1 — PACTA+TRISK is the strongest fit for BIDV’s Decision 263 context. With 8 out of 10 dimensions scored Strong Fit, PACTA is the highest-scored framework in the evaluation. TRISK scores 5 Strong Fit dimensions and is the only framework that translates alignment gaps into borrower-level financial risk metrics (NPV and PD changes). Together, they form a complete analytical pipeline from alignment assessment to stress testing.

Finding 2 — PCAF is the mandatory prerequisite. Already in place as Output 2.1, PCAF provides the emissions accounting foundation that PACTA, TRISK, SBTi, and IFRS S2 all depend on. It scored 7 Strong Fit dimensions and is the lowest-barrier entry point for BIDV’s climate risk capabilities.

Finding 3 — IFRS S2 creates the disclosure demand. With 5 Strong Fit dimensions, IFRS S2 is the disclosure framework that turns analytical outputs into investor-ready reporting. Vietnam’s regulatory trajectory (SBV taxonomy, SSC sustainability disclosure roadmap) is aligned with ISSB adoption. This creates both regulatory pressure and a clear implementation pathway.

Finding 4 — No single framework covers all dimensions. The evaluation confirms that a single-framework approach would leave critical gaps. PACTA does not produce financial risk metrics (requires TRISK). PCAF measures but does not align or stress-test. SBTi validates but does not produce analytical outputs. NGFS provides scenarios but not portfolio-level analysis. The recommended stack is an integrated pipeline where each layer’s outputs are the next layer’s inputs.

Recommendation. BIDV should adopt the three-layer stack:

  • Layer 1 (Prerequisite): Continue using PCAF for financed emissions accounting. Build on the existing Output 2.1 baseline.
  • Layer 2 (Primary analytical): Deploy PACTA for portfolio alignment assessment against PDP8 and NDC targets. Add TRISK for borrower-level transition stress testing. The pacta-trisk repo already demonstrates this pipeline with synthetic Vietnamese bank data.
  • Layer 3 (Disclosure): Build toward IFRS S2-aligned climate disclosure, using PACTA/TRISK analytical outputs as the evidence base for scenario analysis and financed emissions reporting.

Complementary frameworks: Join GFANZ/NZBA for governance and peer learning when ready for a public net-zero commitment. Use NGFS scenarios for more sophisticated macroeconomic stress testing. Consider SBTi FI target validation after PCAF data quality improves.

09 / Risks / Open Questions

Risks / Open Questions

Unresolved Risks

  • Decision 263 quota levels not independently verified. Exact numerical GHG quotas for thermal power, steel, and cement are published in MONRE implementing circulars (Vietnamese-language only). The mapping document’s sector and compliance structure is valid regardless of specific quota numbers, but the credibility of findings would increase with verified quota data.
  • MONRE guidance documents not accessible in English. The MRV methodology, facility size thresholds, and penalty frameworks are documented in Vietnamese-language materials that could not be independently verified. A Vietnamese regulatory review is recommended before final delivery to BIDV.
  • Inherent PACTA+TRISK bias risk. The repo only implements PACTA and TRISK, which creates an unavoidable framing advantage. The evaluation mitigates this with explicit trade-off analysis and documented scenarios where peer frameworks outperform (Section 6 in the comparison document), but an independent validator would strengthen credibility.

Open Questions

  • BIDV’s actual portfolio composition. The recommendation is based on the synthetic MCB portfolio proxy and the assumption of 20+ Decision 263 clients. If BIDV’s actual sector exposure differs materially (e.g., significant oil & gas or automotive lending), the framework recommendation weightings may shift.
  • SBTi FI adoption cost-benefit. The recommendation marks SBTi FI as “consider after PCAF data maturity,” but the cost-benefit threshold for a Vietnamese state-owned bank is unknown. External advisory costing would be needed before a concrete SBTi FI recommendation can be made.
  • Regulatory trajectory uncertainty. IFRS S2 adoption in Vietnam depends on the SSC’s timeline for mandatory sustainability disclosure, which has not been finalized. The recommendations assume continuing regulatory momentum toward ISSB alignment.
